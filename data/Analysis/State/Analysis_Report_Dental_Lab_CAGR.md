Analysis Report: Dental Laboratory Establishment CAGR

Summary: Delaware, Nevada, Idaho, and Montana ranked among the top five states by dental laboratory establishment CAGR in both periods, although most of these states experienced a decline in establishment counts over the long term. The rankings reflect relative changes in establishment count and do not, by themselves, indicate actual market demand or determine whether a state is an attractive location for a new dental laboratory. 

---

1. Work Completed

Question: How have the number of dental laboratory establishments changed over time?

Method:

- Metric: Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR) of dental laboratory establishment counts.
- Data Source : U.S. Census Bureau, County Business Patterns (CBP)
- Industry : NAICS 339116 - Dental Laboratories
- Formula : CAGR = (Ending Value / Beginning Value)^(1/n) − 1
- Long-Term CAGR (2010-2023) :
        (Establishments_2023 / Establishments_2010)^(1/13) − 1
- Recent Five-Year CAGR (2018-2023) :
        (Establishments_2023 / Establishments_2018)^(1/5) − 1

3. Interpretation:
- Delaware ranked first in both the long-term and recent five-year CAGR rankings. Nevada, Idaho, and Montana also appeared among the top five states in both periods, indicating consistency in their relative rankings across the two periods.

- Wyoming appeared in the long-term top five but not in the recent-period leaders. This suggests that the relative growth rankings varied across periods and that recent trends may differ from longer-term patterns.

- These rankings reflect relative differences in dental laboratory establishment CAGR, not necessarily an increase in establishment counts. States with negative CAGR values experienced a decline in establishment counts, while those with positive values experienced growth. Beginning and ending establishment counts should also be reviewed to understand the magnitude of these changes.

- This analysis considers only changes in dental laboratory establishment counts. It does not directly measure market demand, revenue, laboratory capacity, or overall business attractiveness. A decline in establishment counts could indicate reduced competition, but it could also reflect a contracting market.

- The results should therefore be used as an initial screening reference. Dental practice growth, Growth Gap, population trends, market density, and dental expenditure should be considered together before prioritizing states for further investigation.
