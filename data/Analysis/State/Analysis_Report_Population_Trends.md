Analysis Report: Population Trends

Summary: Population CAGR rankings identify states with the fastest relative population growth, while population-change rankings highlight states with the largest absolute increases in population. These metrics provide complementary perspectives on potential market size and growth but do not, by themselves, establish demand for dental laboratory services or determine market attractiveness.

---

1. Work Completed

Question: How have state populations changed over time, and which states experienced the highest population growth rates and the largest absolute population increases?

Method:
- Metric : Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR) and Population change.
- Data Source : U.S. Census Bureau - State Population Estimates
- Formula : CAGR = (Ending Value / Beginning Value)^(1/n) − 1
- Long-Term CAGR (2010-2023) :
        (Population in 2023 / Population in 2010)^(1/13) − 1
- Recent Five-Year CAGR (2018-2023) :
        (Population in 2023 / Population in 2018)^(1/5) − 1
- Population Change (2010-2023) :
        Population in 2023 − Population in 2010

Interpretation:
- daho ranked first in both long-term (2010-2023) and recent five-year (2018-2023) population CAGR, but ranked 19th in absolute population change. This illustrates that a high population growth rate does not necessarily correspond to a large increase in population or a large existing market. Idaho's growth rate should therefore be considered alongside its actual population size and absolute population change.

- Texas and Florida appeared among the top five states in both population CAGR rankings and ranked first and second, respectively, in absolute population change. Their results indicate both strong relative population growth and substantial population increases, making them candidates for further investigation alongside dental-practice and dental-laboratory establishment trends.

- Utah ranked among the top two states in both population CAGR periods but did not appear among the top five states by absolute population change. This suggests that its relative population growth was strong, although its total population increase was smaller than that of the largest-growing states.

- Georgia and North Carolina ranked among the top five states in absolute population change, despite not appearing among the top five in either population CAGR ranking. Their results indicate substantial increases in population in absolute terms and warrant further comparison with dental-practice and dental-laboratory establishment trends.

- Population trends provide context for the potential size and growth of a customer market, but population growth alone does not measure dental-service utilization or demand for dental laboratory services.

- Population estimates may reflect different data vintages across the analysis period. Therefore, the calculated growth rates should be interpreted as indicative trends.
