## 1. Screening Metrics

States were identified using the following metrics:

- Market Size: Population (2023), Dental Practice Establishments (2023), and Total Dental Services Expenditure (2020, absolute value).

- Establishment Trends: Dental Practice CAGR, Dental Laboratory CAGR, and Growth Gap, for both long-term (2010–2023) and recent five-year (2018–2023) periods.

- Population Trends: Long-term population CAGR, recent five-year population CAGR, and absolute population change (2010–2023).

- Market Density (2023): Population per Dental Practice, Population per Dental Laboratory, and Dental Practices per Dental Laboratory.

- Dental Services Expenditure Trends (2010–2020): Total expenditure CAGR and per capita expenditure CAGR.

## 3. Screening Criteria and Assessment

### 3.1. State Selection Framework

- The preliminary state shortlist was developed by considering market size and growth potential together, rather than selecting states solely based on individual metric rankings.

The screening criteria were organized into the following areas:

- Market size:
    Population, dental practice establishments, and total dental services expenditure were considered to assess the scale of the existing market.

- Dental Practice Growth:
    Long-term and recent growth in dental practice establishments was used to assess changes in the potential customer base.

- Population Growth:
    Long-term and recent population trends, including absolute population change, were considered to evaluate changes in the broader market base.

- Dental Laboratory Trends:
    Changes in laboratory establishment counts and CAGR were reviewed to understand the existing supply structure. A high ranking was not automatically treated as positive growth or evidence of unmet demand.

- Supporting Indicators:
    Dental services expenditure trends, Growth Gap, and Market Density were used as supplementary indicators to provide additional context.

- The selection process considered the combined evidence across these criteria, along with each state's limitations and conflicting signals. Market size and growth potential served as the primary considerations, while supporting indicators helped contextualize the results. No single metric was treated as sufficient to determine a state's suitability.

- The five candidate states therefore represent a preliminary shortlist for further investigation, not a definitive ranking of the most attractive markets.

### 3.4. Cross Metric Trade-Offs and Alternative Candidates

#### Cross Metric Trade-Offs

- **Large Market Size vs Recent Growth** : California and New York rank in the top five across all three market-size metrics, while Pennsylvania ranks in population and Illinois ranks in dental practice establishments and total expenditure. California's recent population CAGR was negative (-0.13%), highlighting a trade-off between market size and recent growth.

- **Practice Growth vs Laboratory Trends** : Idaho and Nevada show strong dental practice growth, but laboratory counts declined from 62 to 55 and from 63 to 60, respectively. Florida and Texas combine large markets with declining laboratory counts (556 to 424 and 344 to 277, respectively). Delaware shows growth in both practice and laboratory counts, but its laboratory base remains small at 13 establishments in 2023.

- **Growth Gap and Market Density** : North Dakota and South Dakota combine increasing dental practice counts with declining dental laboratory counts. However, several other states with high Growth Gaps, including Iowa, New Jersey, Oregon, Wisconsin, and Kansas, experienced flat or declining dental practice counts. Market Density ratios, including population per dental laboratory and dental practices per laboratory, provide additional context on the relative distribution of establishments but do not directly measure unmet demand, laboratory capacity, or geographic accessibility. Therefore, high Growth Gaps and Market Density ratios should be treated as indicators for further investigation rather than direct evidence of market opportunity.

- **Dental Services Expenditure as Supporting Evidence** : Florida and Montana show strong results in both total and per capita expenditure CAGR. South Dakota ranks first in both measures (5.77% and 4.88%), but its smaller establishment base should be considered. Expenditure growth indicates expansion in dental-service spending, not direct dental laboratory revenue or outsourced case demand.

#### Alternative Candidates for Comparison

- **Montana** : Strong dental practice and expenditure growth, but declining laboratory counts and a smaller market base.
- **Delaware** : Strong recent dental practice and population growth, with laboratory counts increasing from 9 to 13.
- **South Dakota** : Top-five total and per capita expenditure CAGR, with increasing dental practice counts but a relatively small establishment base.
- **New York** : Large market size across all three market-size metrics, but fewer top-five growth signals than Texas and Florida.

These alternatives will be compared with the preliminary candidates before the final five states are selected for county-level analysis.
