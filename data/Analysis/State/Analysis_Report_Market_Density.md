Analysis Report: Market Density

Summary: Market density ratios were calculated for 2023, the latest common year available across the selected population, dental practice, and dental laboratory establishment datasets. While CAGR analyses examine changes over time, market density provides a snapshot of the relative market structure in a single year. These ratios offer supplementary insights into potential customer coverage but do not directly measure unmet demand, competition intensity, or laboratory capacity.

---

1. Work Completed

Question: How do states differ in population per dental practice, population per dental laboratory, and dental practices per dental laboratory?

Method:
- Metric: Population per Dental Practice
          Population per Dental Laboratory
          Dental Practices per Dental Laboratory
- Data Sources:
          U.S. Census Bureau - State Population Estimates
          U.S. Census Bureau - County Business Patterns (CBP)
- Industries:
          NAICS 621210 - Offices of Dentists
          NAICS 339116 - Dental Laboratories
- Reference Year : 2023
- Rationale : 2023 was selected as the latest common year available across the selected datasets to compare population
              and establishment counts on a consistent basis.
- Formula:
    - Population per Dental Practice : Population (2023) / Dental Practice Establishments (2023)
    - Population per Dental Laboratory : Population (2023) / Dental Laboratory Establishments (2023)
    - Dental Practices per Dental Laboratory :
            Dental Practice Establishments (2023) / Dental Laboratory Establishments (2023)

Interpretation:
- West Virginia, Alabama, Delaware, Rhode Island, and Arkansas ranked among the top five states by population per dental practice. These ratios do not account for geographic distribution, patient travel patterns, or differences in practice size and capacity.

- South Carolina, North Dakota, Texas, Arkansas, and Pennsylvania ranked among the top five states by population per dental laboratory. These ratios can be sensitive to small establishment counts, particularly in states with relatively few dental laboratories.

- North Dakota, South Carolina, New Jersey, Texas, and Connecticut ranked among the top five states by dental practices per dental laboratory. A higher ratio indicates more dental practice establishments relative to dental laboratories, but it does not necessarily mean that each laboratory has more customers or receives more cases.

- South Carolina, North Dakota, and Texas appeared among the top five in both population per dental laboratory and dental practices per dental laboratory. Arkansas appeared among the top five in both population per dental practice and population per dental laboratory. These overlaps provide useful starting points for comparing market structure across states, but they do not establish that these states are the most attractive locations for a new dental laboratory.

- These three ratios are mathematically related rather than fully independent indicators. Therefore, overlapping rankings should not be interpreted as three separate pieces of evidence for market attractiveness.
