Analysis Report: Growth Gap between Dental Practice and Dental Laboratory Establishment CAGR

Summary: The Growth Gap is calculated as dental practice establishment CAGR minus dental laboratory establishment CAGR. A large positive gap indicates that dental practice establishments grew faster, or declined less, than dental laboratory establishments. These results identify states for further investigation but do not, by themselves, establish unmet demand or market attractiveness.

---

1. Work Completed

Question: Which states have the highest Growth Gap between dental practice and dental laboratory establishment CAGR?

Method:
- Metric : Growth Gap between dental practice and dental laboratory CAGR.
- Data Source : U.S. Census Bureau - County Business Patterns (CBP).
- Industries :
    - NAICS 621210 - Office of Dentists.
    - NAICS 339116 - Dental Laboratories.
- Formula : Growth Gap = Dental Practice CAGR − Dental Laboratory CAGR
- Long-Term Growth Gap (2010-2023) :
    Dental Practice CAGR (2010–2023) − Dental Laboratory CAGR (2010–2023)
- Recent Five-Year Growth Gap (2018-2023) :
    Dental Practice CAGR (2018–2023) − Dental Laboratory CAGR (2018–2023)
- Unit : Percentage points (pp)

---

2. Results

Analysis:
Growth_Gap_Long_Term

Period:
2010-2023

Result:
1. Iowa / 4.56 %p 
2. New Jersey / 4.34 %p
3. Oregon / 4.34 %p
4. Wisconsin / 4.29 %p
5. Kansas / 4.12 %p

Analysis:
Growth_Gap_Recent

Period:
2018-2023

Result:
1. North Dakota / 9.50 %p
2. Alaska / 8.67 %p
3. Oregon / 7.24 %p
4. Wisconsin / 6.45 %p
5. South Dakota / 5.80 %p

---

Interpretation:
- Oregon and Wisconsin ranked among the top five states by Growth Gap in both periods, maintaining relatively high rankings across the long-term (2010-2023) and recent five-year (2018-2023) periods. Iowa, New Jersey, and Kansas appeared among the long-term leaders, while North Dakota, Alaska, and South Dakota appeared among the recent-period leaders.

- All states in the top five rankings had positive Growth Gaps in both periods. A positive Growth Gap indicates that dental practice establishments grew faster, or declined less, than dental laboratory establishments. However, the ranking alone does not show the magnitude of the difference. The actual CAGR values and underlying establishment counts should also be reviewed.

- Growth Gap measures the difference between dental practice and dental laboratory establishment growth rates; it does not directly measure unmet demand for dental laboratory services. A positive Growth Gap may reflect faster growth (or a slower decline) in dental practices relative to dental laboratories, including situations where laboratory establishments are declining. Growth rate may also be volatile when establishment counts are small, so the gap should be interpreted alongside the underlying establishment counts.
